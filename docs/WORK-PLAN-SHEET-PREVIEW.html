Work plan sheet — the Excel view

Same project, same numbers as the Plan-vs-actual tab. This is the shape you already print and pin up: rows of work, columns of days, a shaded cell for every day the job is meant to run — and a second shaded row underneath for what actually happened.

1 · What was wrong with the chart

Nothing, as a chart. But an Excel work plan is not a chart — it is a register with a calendar bolted to the side, and that is why it survives being printed, signed and pinned to a wall. Three things it has that the chart did not:

  • Columns. A number people can say out loud (“item 4 is late”), the phase, the responsible person, start, finish, days, hours, weight, per cent, and a remark column that carries the real story.
  • Days you can count. A shaded cell per working day, Sundays greyed, today marked — not a bar you have to measure against a gridline.
  • A bottom band. Plan % and Actual % per week, and the cumulative pair underneath. That band is the S-curve — the curve is just those two rows drawn.

4 · Printing and export

  • Print lays it out A3 landscape with the header row repeating on every page, the buttons and commentary hidden, and the day columns tightened so a ten-week project still fits across. A4 landscape works for anything up to about six weeks in day mode, or a year in week mode.
  • Export to Excel writes the same sheet as a real .xlsx — the columns as columns, the shaded days as filled cells, the bottom band as formulas — so the file you send on can still be edited by somebody who does not use the app.

The point of the export is not to leave the app. It is so the plan can go to a contractor, a vendor or a meeting that runs on Excel, without anybody having to rebuild it by hand and then keep two versions in step.

One decision, and it is about weight. The bottom band and the S-curve both need to know what each task is worth. Two honest answers:
  • Man-hours — what I have used here. Free, already on every task, and it measures effort. It undervalues a task that is mostly waiting: “material receive” is 120 hours of nothing happening.
  • Cost — closer to what a manager means by progress, but it means typing a value on every task and keeping it current.
My recommendation: hours by default, with a cost column that overrides the weight when it is filled in. That way the sheet works on day one and gets better if anybody wants it to.
This does not replace the chart — it sits beside it. The work-plan sheet is what you print, hand out and sign. The Plan-vs-actual chart is what you look at when somebody asks whether the finish date still holds. Same rows, same dates, one more tab.
