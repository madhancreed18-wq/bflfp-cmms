Idea B — how it differs, and the hold rule

The hold rule

Stage 1ConditionStage 2 shows
HoldNo due date on the jobPending due date — the planner still owes this job a date
HoldDue date set by the plannerWait for action — parked until the part or the supplier arrives

In this layout the rule is not a label in a corner — the wait is the last node of the spine, and it carries the date box that clears it. Code touchpoints are unchanged: stage_for() and set_stage() in server/db.py take due_date, and stagePair() in static/index.html mirrors the branch.

What makes this different from idea A

  1. One column, not two. The same page works on a phone and on the planner's monitor without a second layout.
  2. The record head reads like F-SP-ENG02-03. Eight fixed fields in the order the paper form uses, so what is on screen and what gets printed agree. A missing due date turns that one cell red instead of hiding in a list.
  3. Everything else is chronological. Report, assignment, start, hold, work report, photos, sign-off and the chat are nodes on one spine with a time gutter — the job reads as what happened, which is how a technician tells it.
  4. The action lives where the story stops. The open node is the live one, tinted and pulsing, holding the exact control the job is waiting for.
  5. Actions dock at the bottom instead of a toolbar at the top — thumb reach on a phone, and the primary action changes with the state.
