PM plan — finish one building before starting the next

Measured against the real BFLPC and BFL registers. Nothing has been changed yet — this is for your decision.

1 · Why Tower A comes up every day

Each frequency plans its own route, and the routes do not agree with each other.

  • Weekly work is dealt across the six working days, building by building — a six-day round that repeats every week.
  • Monthly work is dealt across the twenty-six working days of the month, building by building — a completely separate round.
  • Quarterly, half-yearly and yearly are not spread at all. Every machine of that frequency falls on one day.

So on any given day the weekly round is in one building and the monthly round is in another. Here is the real BFLPC plan, first two weeks:

3 · The proposal

One route for the whole day, shared by every frequency.

  1. Add up how much work each building really carries per week — a weekly machine counts 1, a monthly machine counts ¼, a quarterly &frac1;⁄₁₃, and so on.
  2. Walk the buildings in order and cut that total into six equal days. A building bigger than a day runs over into the next day and no further; a building too small to fill a day shares it with its neighbours.
  3. Every job — whatever its frequency — is placed on a day that already belongs to its building. Long-cycle work is then spread across the weeks of its own cycle inside that day, so a quarterly machine still comes round every quarter, but on the Wednesday the crew is already in Tower A.

Nothing about how often a machine is serviced changes. Drag-and-drop moves, holidays and the Sunday-off rule all keep working as they do today.

6 · Honest limitations

  • Monday stays mixed. Boiler, Liquid Room, Extruder, Canteen and the compressor room together only add up to one day's work, so they share it. On BFL that Monday round grows from 16 days a year to 28 — it is the price of taking those little rooms out of every other day, and it is one planned round rather than a stray job here and there.
  • It levels by machine count, not by minutes. A day is 77 jobs, not 77 equal jobs. If you want it balanced by checklist length instead, that is a small change once the estimated minutes are on the checklists.
  • A machine's building comes from its location field. BFL still mixes WH and Ware House as two buildings; tidying those in the register tightens the route.
  • Jobs already created keep their dates. The new route applies from the day it is switched on forward; nothing that has been planned or worked is disturbed.
