Can the crew actually take this project?

The S-curve says how much is earned. The timeline says which task is late. Neither says whether the people exist to do the work — which is what made the foundations slip in the first place.

1 · The question

A project plan is a promise about dates. Whether it can be kept is a question about people — and the same four technicians who install the bagging line are the ones who owe 302 PM jobs a week and whatever breaks on Tuesday.

Every hour is already in the app. The PM route knows its own minutes. Breakdown hours are twelve weeks of history. Project hours and who does them are on the task. Nothing new has to be typed — the three just have to be added up in the same place.

6 · What it costs to build

Three sums the app can already do, one screen to show them:

  • PM hours per person per week — the PM route already assigns machines to days; each machine already carries estimated minutes. Group by technician and week.
  • Project hours per person per week — a task's hours spread across the days of its own span, credited to the person in who.
  • Breakdown allowance — that person's average CM + BD hours over the last twelve weeks. Measured, not guessed, and it updates itself.

The one number somebody has to agree: what a technician can actually give in a week. Not the payroll hours — the honest number after walking between towers, waiting for a permit and standing in the morning meeting. Set it once per person; it is the line everything else is judged against.

One decision. When a week goes over, what should the app do?
  • Warn only. Colour the week and leave it to the planner. Honest, and easy to ignore.
  • Refuse the assignment. The assign board will not let a job be dropped on someone already over. Safe, and it will be fought about the first week.
  • Warn, and offer the swap. When you assign into a full week, the app names who in the same trade has room and offers to move a PM job across. My recommendation — it turns a red number into one press.
